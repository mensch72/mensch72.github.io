Steerability of a robot fleet by one overseer

Mirrored axioms and their relation to human power

Summary

Abel et al. (2025) call the influence of observations on an agent’s actions plasticity and define it by a directed-information quantity. The note asks what the axiomatic approach of Heitzig and Potham (2026) to human power gives for the mirrored question: how well can one overseer steer each of several robots with fixed policies? It then relates three quantities: the overseer’s power over the world, the robots’ own power, and the robots’ steerability.

Quantities

Let \(p(g)\) be the largest probability with which the overseer can bring about a behaviour goal \(g\). With \(\mathcal G_r\) a partition of robot \(r\)’s behaviours and \(\mathcal G_{\mathcal R}\) the conjunctions, the steerability of robot \(r\) and the joint steerability of the fleet \(\mathcal R\) are

\[S_r=\log_2\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_r}p(g)^\zeta,\qquad S_{\mathcal R}=\log_2\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_{\mathcal R}}p(g)^\zeta .\]

For world goals \(g\), let \(p_h(g)\) be the overseer’s capability, \(q_g\) the fleet’s own capability if it pursued \(g\) as a rational coalition, and \(t_g\) the largest probability with which the overseer can make the fleet behave as it would if it pursued \(g\). Human power, robot power and instructable power are

\[I_h=\log_2\sum_g p_h(g)^\zeta,\qquad I_{\mathcal R}=\log_2\sum_g q_g^\zeta,\qquad L=\log_2\sum_g (q_g t_g)^\zeta .\]

The overseer is assumed to affect the world goals only through the robots’ joint behaviour.

Axioms and functional forms

  • The power axioms carry over by renaming and give the forms above with the same six parameters. The fleet-level aggregate is \[F=-\tfrac1\xi\log_2\sum_r 2^{-\xi S_r},\qquad \min_r S_r-\tfrac{\log_2 n}{\xi}\le F\le\min_r S_r .\]
  • A null axiom contradicts Reliability Preference. If all robots that ignore the overseer must have the same steerability, the exponent must be \(\zeta=1\), which the power axioms exclude. For one-shot commands and singleton goals, the \(\zeta=1\) measure is the maximal leakage of Issa et al. (2020).
  • Least-robot focus. Losing one bit of one robot’s steerability is uncompensable by \(k\) other robots if and only if \(k\le 2^\xi-1\). Uncompensability for all fleet sizes forces \(F=\min_r S_r\) and gives up strict monotonicity.
  • Granularity. \(F\) is invariant under replacing a group of robots by a representative with their aggregate steerability. It is not invariant under merging robots into a coalition with joint behaviours. That invariance would force \(F=S_{\mathcal R}\), which is a sum for independent robots and violates inequality aversion.
  • Without anonymity of robots, the aggregate becomes \(F=-\tfrac1\xi\log_2\sum_r 2^{-\xi(S_r-\omega_r)}\) with robot-specific offsets \(\omega_r\), a soft minimum of steerability surplus over a requirement. Unweighted inequality aversion forces equal offsets. If the offset depends on a robot only through its power \(I_r\), single-robot composition additivity gives \(\omega_r=\kappa I_r\) with one new parameter \(\kappa\ge0\).

Joint and individual steerability

\(S_{\mathcal R}=\sum_r S_r\) for independently commanded robots, and \(S_{\mathcal R}\le\sum_r S_r\) whenever the robots’ behaviours are independent under every overseer strategy. Under a command bottleneck \(S_{\mathcal R}\) is much smaller than the sum. With randomness shared between robots it can exceed the sum. The individual steerabilities therefore do not determine the joint one.

Human power, robot power and steerability

  • Bounds through joint steerability. If the outcome is a deterministic function of the joint behaviour with at most \(m\) behaviour cells per world goal, then for \(\zeta\ge1\) \[S_{\mathcal R}-\log_2 m\;\le\;I_h\;\le\;S_{\mathcal R}+(\zeta-1)\log_2 m .\]
  • Power implies total, not least, steerability. Under independence and \(\zeta=1\), some robot has \(S_r\ge I_h/n\). Human power implies no lower bound on the least steerable robot or on \(F\). In the other direction, a high \(F\) gives human power only if the robots are also jointly steerable and their behaviour matters for the world goals.
  • Robot power bounds human power. \(I_h\le I_{\mathcal R}\), and for \(\zeta=1\) also \(I_h\le\min(I_{\mathcal R},S_{\mathcal R})\).
  • A triangle inequality in effective numbers of goals. For \(\zeta\ge1\) and singleton behaviour goals, \[|\mathcal B|-2^{I_h}\;\le\;\big(|\mathcal B|-2^{I_{\mathcal R}}\big)+\big(|\mathcal B|-2^{S_{\mathcal R}}\big),\] where \(\mathcal B\) is the set of joint behaviours. The corresponding inequality in bits fails, because \(S_{\mathcal R}\) counts steerable behaviours whether or not the robots’ power runs through them.

Targeted steerability

Weighting each world goal by the robots’ capability for it restores the product rule. The targeted steerability is

\[S^{\mathrm t}=L-I_{\mathcal R}=\log_2\sum_g w_g\,t_g^\zeta\le 0,\qquad w_g\propto q_g^\zeta,\]

and it gives a triangle inequality in bits, \(I_h\ge L=I_{\mathcal R}+S^{\mathrm t}\). The uncontrolled power \(U=2^{I_{\mathcal R}}-2^{L}\) is the part of the robots’ effective number of goals that the overseer cannot direct, and \(0\le U\le|\mathcal B|-2^{S_{\mathcal R}}\).

The anonymous aggregate of the per-robot values \(S^{\mathrm t}_r=L_r-I_r\) equals the non-anonymous aggregate above with \(\kappa=1\) and \(S_r\) replaced by \(L_r\). A robot whose behaviour does not affect the goals has \(S^{\mathrm t}_r=0\) and does not lower this fleet value, however unsteerable it is.

Limitations

The overseer is fully rational and capabilities are undiscounted. With a boundedly rational overseer the bounds on \(I_h\) can fail. Direct effects of the overseer on the world and aggregation over time are not treated. The targeted steerability is a ratio, so a one-bit gap counts the same for a weak and a strong robot. An aggregate of the absolute uncontrolled powers is an alternative that the axioms do not decide. How one robot’s capability in a fleet depends on the assumed behaviour of the others is left open.
