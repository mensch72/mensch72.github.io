Unstated voters: how recent voting papers state their behavioural assumptions

social choice
computational social choice
voting
meta-research
Published

November 17, 2026

Status: AI-assisted draft

This is a coding study of 150 voting papers from 2021–2025 and a 4-page note on its results. All screening, coding and adjudication was done by language models (Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.5) under my supervision. The evidence quotes were checked automatically against the paper texts (98.8 % found verbatim), but no code has yet been checked by a human: a hand-check of 15 papers is prepared but not done.

Note: PDF, 4 pages · Data: dataset.xlsx (codes and evidence for all 150 papers) · Code and full pipeline: github.com/mensch72/comsoc-rationality-review (branch claude/wonderful-hopper-jsla1z)

Summary

My impression was that voting papers are rigorous inside their formal models but often leave the behavioural interpretation unstated. To test it, I drew a stratified random sample of 150 papers on voting from 2021–2025. The papers come from AAMAS, IJCAI, AAAI, EC, the COMSOC workshop, Social Choice and Welfare, Games and Economic Behavior, Journal of Economic Theory and Mathematical Social Sciences.

Each paper was coded with a codebook that was frozen after a 10-paper pilot. The codebook asks:

  • how the paper relates submitted ballots to voters’ preferences and utilities;
  • whether each type of agent is modelled as strategic, and what it knows;
  • whether welfare or real-world claims depend on assumptions the paper does not state.

Two model runs coded every paper independently, and a third adjudicated their disagreements. Estimates are design-weighted, with stratified-bootstrap confidence intervals.

The results support the impression only in part.

Main results

The figures below are design-weighted, with 95 % CIs in brackets.

  • About 60 % of papers treat voters as sincere in at least one analysis without giving a reason (59 % [52–66]). Most of them do not state the assumption at all. The figure barely depends on which coder is used (57–62 %).
  • About half make a welfare or real-world claim that rests on an assumption they do not state (48 % [41–56]). Typically the missing assumption is that submitted ballots reflect what voters hold. This figure does depend on the coder: 45 % with one coder alone, 66 % with the other.
  • Most papers do say how ballots relate to preferences (69 % [63–76]). Where utilities appear alongside ordinal or approval ballots, 83 % [73–92] define the link. Treating preferences, ballots and utilities as one object is therefore a minority practice, concentrated in algorithmic and axiomatic papers.
  • Purely procedural papers are rare (7 %). Only 1 paper in 150 says explicitly that it takes a procedural reading.

Some items fall below κ = 0.40 between the two coders, and the note marks them as unreliable. It also gives the results by venue type and paper type, six named examples of good practice, and five randomly drawn, anonymised examples of claims that rest on unstated assumptions. It includes a calibration case (one of my own papers) and a short reporting checklist.

Files and reproduction

  • unstated-voters-note.pdf: the note.
  • dataset.xlsx: one row per paper, with bibliographic data, the open version coded, codes, locations, quotes of at most 12 words, and weights.
  • Results tables (CSV):
    • Estimates: outcomes, by venue type, by paper type, stakeholders.
    • Reliability: items, derived measures, coder sensitivity.
    • Sample: replacements, eligibility rates.
  • scripts.zip: the sampling, coding, merge, quote-check and analysis scripts (Python 3; the analysis needs openpyxl).

The scripts expect the layout of the study repository, which also holds the protocol, the codebook, the frame, the sample and all codings.

To recompute every estimate, CI and reliability figure, and the dataset, from the final codings:

git clone -b claude/wonderful-hopper-jsla1z https://github.com/mensch72/comsoc-rationality-review
cd comsoc-rationality-review && python3 scripts/analyze.py

The coder-sensitivity figures come from re-running analyze.py with the final codings replaced by pass A’s or pass B’s codings.

Re-coding from scratch is a heavier job:

  • It needs the paper texts, which are not redistributed. The dataset gives the URL of each open version used.
  • It needs access to the models through OpenRouter (scripts/api_code.py).
  • Model outputs will not be identical on a re-run.
